# Governance

This project is community-shaped and founder-owned.

The community is invited to propose, discuss, review and vote on selected decisions. Legal ownership, intellectual-property control and final responsibility remain with the project founder unless a future legal structure explicitly changes that.

## Core Principles

1. **The community should have a real voice.** Community participation must influence selected project decisions in visible and documented ways.
2. **Votes do not bypass reality.** Legal, technical, financial, safety and availability checks may invalidate an otherwise popular option.
3. **Every important decision leaves a record.** Major decisions should show what was proposed, how it was evaluated, what was decided and why.
4. **Authorship must be preserved.** A contribution may become part of the project without erasing the identity of the person who created it.
5. **No hidden administrative power.** Important moderation and governance actions should be explainable and reviewable.
6. **The project may evolve.** Governance rules can improve, but changes should be documented rather than silently replacing history.

## Decision Classes

### A. Community Selection

The community helps choose between eligible options after screening.

Examples:

- project name;
- selected visual directions;
- community events;
- selected feature priorities;
- educational initiatives.

Typical flow:

`Proposal -> Discussion -> Eligibility Review -> Shortlist -> Vote -> Final Validation -> Decision Record`

### B. Community Consultation

The community gives structured feedback, but the result is not automatically binding.

Examples:

- gameplay balance;
- roadmap priorities;
- documentation structure;
- accessibility priorities.

### C. Technical / Maintainer Decision

Decisions that require specialized implementation knowledge may be handled by maintainers after public discussion when practical.

Examples:

- architecture;
- code organization;
- security fixes;
- performance tradeoffs;
- dependency changes.

### D. Reserved Legal / Ownership Decision

Some decisions cannot be delegated by a community vote.

Examples:

- trademark filing;
- contracts;
- ownership transfer;
- intellectual-property enforcement;
- financial obligations;
- compliance with law;
- publication of confidential or personal information.

The community may still be consulted, but the founder or legally responsible entity makes the final decision.

## Standard Proposal Process

### 1. Proposal

A proposal should explain:

- the problem or opportunity;
- the proposed change;
- expected benefits;
- possible risks or tradeoffs;
- whether it affects existing decisions;
- who is willing to help implement it.

### 2. Discussion

The community discusses the proposal. Criticism should address the idea, not the person.

### 3. Eligibility Review

Before voting, the proposal may be checked for:

- project fit;
- legal conflicts;
- technical feasibility;
- originality and third-party rights;
- cost or operational impact;
- privacy and safety concerns.

### 4. Shortlist

When multiple valid options exist, a shortlist may be created before the final vote.

### 5. Vote

Each official vote must clearly state:

- what is being decided;
- who can vote;
- opening and closing dates;
- eligible options;
- whether the result is binding or advisory;
- the validation conditions that still apply.

Changing these rules after voting starts requires a public explanation.

### 6. Final Validation

The preferred option is checked against any remaining legal, technical, availability or safety requirements.

For example, a winning project name is not official until required conflict, domain, social-identity and trademark checks are completed.

### 7. Decision Record

The final outcome is added to [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md), including the date, result and reasoning.

## Founder and Maintainer Responsibilities

The founder is responsible for protecting project continuity, ownership, legal identity and core principles.

Maintainers are expected to:

- explain major decisions;
- disclose relevant conflicts of interest;
- preserve contribution history;
- avoid using privileges for personal advantage;
- apply moderation rules consistently;
- separate personal disagreement from rule enforcement.

## Community Responsibilities

Participants are expected to:

- argue ideas with evidence and respect;
- avoid harassment and personal attacks;
- disclose third-party materials used in contributions;
- avoid manipulating votes or creating fake support;
- respect confidential information and personal data;
- accept that not every good idea can be implemented immediately.

## Ownership and Contribution

Community participation does not automatically create ownership, partnership, shares or a right to project revenue.

Specific contribution and licensing terms will be published before code and asset contributions formally open.

Attribution should remain part of the historical record whenever appropriate, even when commercial or economic rights are separately licensed or assigned under valid agreements.

## Governance History

Governance itself should have versioned history. Significant changes to this document should be proposed, discussed and recorded in [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md).
